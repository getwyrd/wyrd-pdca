feat(dst): prove TiKV metadata swap holds consistency (M4.6, #257)

M4 moves the metadata store from the deterministic redb/in-memory fake to
a real distributed TiKV cluster, behind an unchanged MetadataStore trait.
The deterministic simulation proves the commit protocol against the fake,
but it cannot show that the fake matches the real store: the production
TiKV commit awaits network I/O between the precondition re-read and the
terminal commit, an interleaving the single-transaction redb backend never
exhibits. Until now nothing exercised that window against the real backend,
so a lost update or torn commit introduced by the swap could ship unseen.

This adds the test-only evidence that closes the gap, without touching a
byte of production source or the trait:

- A live Tier-1 consistency scenario drives the real TiKV commit path on a
  real >=3-replica Raft group while the region leader is symmetrically and
  bidirectionally isolated mid-run. Two or more concurrent writers contend
  the same version cell across the fault window, and read-after-commit,
  exactly-once convergence, and no-lost-update are asserted as independent
  signals across the heal. A peer-side fault-effect oracle (PD heartbeat
  freshness) fails the leg if the injected partition was a no-op, and the
  partition self-heals on every path.
- A Tier-1 multi-key atomic create/rename/delete integration leg and a
  Tier-2 single-node real-I/O leg exercise the swap end to end.
- Pure quorum, fault-effect, and no-lost-update arithmetic runs in the
  whole-tree gate with hand-computed expectations, and a redb coverage
  seed narrows the concurrent-CAS classification the sibling concurrency
  campaign leaves unasserted.

The live legs are opt-in (endpoint-gated, ignored by default) and skip
cleanly with no cluster, so the ordinary offline build stays green; the
privileged tier runner stands the cluster up and executes them.

Fixes #257
