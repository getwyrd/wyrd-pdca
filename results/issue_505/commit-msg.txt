feat(505): accept SDK-default checksum-trailer streaming PUTs

A default-configured modern S3 client (aws-cli, boto3, current aws-sdk)
uploads with the aws-chunked checksum-trailer framing: an
x-amz-content-sha256 of STREAMING-AWS4-HMAC-SHA256-PAYLOAD-TRAILER or
STREAMING-UNSIGNED-PAYLOAD-TRAILER, plus a declared x-amz-checksum-*
trailer after the terminating zero-length chunk. The gateway's SigV4
layer keeps a deliberately closed sentinel set — never admit a framing
the decoder cannot fully consume and verify — and the decoder could not
consume trailer bytes, so both trailer sentinels were refused with 403.
A stock `aws s3 cp` against `wyrd s3` therefore failed outright.

Restore compatibility the invariant-preserving way: make the trailer
framing fully consumable and verified, then admit it — never the other
way around.

- The aws-chunked decoder now consumes the trailer section after the
  zero-length chunk (bounded, fail-closed on malformed framing),
  verifies the trailer signature for the signed variant against AWS's
  published trailing-header string-to-sign (pinned to AWS's own
  aws-c-auth worked example), and validates the declared checksum
  against the bytes actually streamed — never consumed-and-trusted.
- Supported algorithms: crc32 (in-tree IEEE table — the aws-cli/boto3
  default), crc32c (the workspace's already-vetted crate, no new
  dependency), and sha256. Anything else, crc64nvme included, is
  refused before any body is read.
- The declaration headers are part of the contract: a consumed trailer
  name must match x-amz-trailer, and a declared
  x-amz-decoded-content-length must match the decoded byte count.
- A checksum mismatch is 400 BadDigest and the object is never
  committed; a malformed trailer section (bad base64, an undeclared
  name, garbage after the block) is a 400, never a silent accept; a
  forged trailer signature stays a 403.

The plain STREAMING-AWS4-HMAC-SHA256-PAYLOAD path of the gateway
introduced in 47bd35c13c3d27c17ac4db8d748b85403d266934 is unchanged and
stays green. The former refusal tests flip to acceptance tests, and a
new wire test drives both trailer sentinels end-to-end over the real
listener — byte-identical round-trips for all three algorithms plus
every fail-closed edge.
