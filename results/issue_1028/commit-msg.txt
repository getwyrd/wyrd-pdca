core: classify a part commit that lost, and clean up after it

When two writers race for the same multipart upload, a part commit can
lose: another upload of the same part number lands first, a repair
rewrites the old part, the upload is aborted, or a failed Complete rolls
the upload back. Until now the attempt stopped on any such conflict and
kept its in-flight slot. While the upload stays open nothing else frees
that slot, so repeated losses could use up the upload's slots until the
session timed out.

The attempt now re-reads the part, the upload and its own slot, and
decides from what it finds. If the upload is still open (a rival won, the
prior part was repaired, or the upload was rolled back to a newer epoch),
it releases its slot and schedules its own data for deletion in one
write, and answers OperationAborted so the client can upload the part
again. If the upload was aborted or deleted, it answers NoSuchUpload and
leaves the rest to the cleanup that already handles those uploads. A
conflict nothing explains is retried. A record that does not decode, or
a state no legal sequence produces, stops the attempt without writing.

Every loop has a fixed bound: four commit rounds and four compensation
rounds, and the lease is checked before each later round. A re-upload
whose deletion record key is already taken stops with that error instead
of reporting a clean cleanup.

Unknown outcomes from the store still stop the attempt as before; #1029
and #1030 handle them. Nothing calls this code in production until the
S3 UploadPart verb is wired (#669), so clients see no change yet.
