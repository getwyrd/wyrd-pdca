Serve grpc.health.v1 liveness/readiness probes from the d-server

A deployment supervisor (systemd, Kubernetes, a load balancer) had no way
to ask a wyrd d-server "are you alive, and ready to serve?": the store's
own health() self-check existed but was exposed through no probe, so the
only outside signal was process existence — an unhealthy node kept
receiving traffic, and the first evidence was errors on the client's
side. A grpc.health.v1 Check against the server returned UNIMPLEMENTED.

The d-server now answers the standard gRPC health-checking protocol, so
any supervisor that already speaks grpc.health.v1 works without
wyrd-specific tooling:

- Readiness mirrors the backing store's own health(), re-read on a
  bounded cadence (3s default, overridable): Healthy and Degraded read
  SERVING (a degraded store still serves), Unhealthy reads NOT_SERVING,
  and an Err from health() itself also reads NOT_SERVING — fail closed,
  because a store that cannot even report its health must not read as
  ready. Until the first successful read the status is NOT_SERVING, so
  an early probe never reads ready by default. The overall (empty-name)
  service is the liveness signal: the process answers at all.

- The probe listens on its own stable, operator-configurable address
  (--health-bind, default 127.0.0.1:50052 beside the data plane's
  127.0.0.1:50051), served by its own unlayered transport outside the
  data plane's admission stack. A probe therefore still answers while
  the data plane is saturated at its admission bound: overload is not
  unreadiness — a probe shed as RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED would make a
  supervisor restart an overloaded-but-healthy node and amplify the
  overload it reacted to.

- At the library level the probe is opt-in (no bind configured means no
  probe socket), so in-process callers that spin several servers are not
  forced onto one fixed port; the deployable wyrd d-server role always
  enables it, defaulting to the stable address above, and prints the
  probe address at startup.

One new dependency, tonic-health, version-matched to tonic 0.14 — the
standard proto reused rather than a bespoke health service.
