server: check metadata consistency under concurrent clients

Wyrd's metadata store advertises read-your-writes and monotonic-read
guarantees (ADR-0015), but nothing exercised them under genuinely concurrent
access — one client overwriting a key while another reads the same key. The
existing consistency observer drove a single client sequentially, so its
recorded history had no overlapping real-time spans and could not show whether
concurrent operations stay consistent. A regression that let concurrent clients
observe a stale, torn, or resurrected read on the mutable metadata register
could ship with no automated signal.

This adds a concurrent workload that runs two or more real S3 clients against
the in-process gateway, merges what each client observed into one
real-time-ordered per-client history, and checks the store's session
guarantees on it: an indeterminate outcome (a timeout or 5xx, whose effect is
unknown) is never treated as a definite success or failure, and a read that
goes backward or resurrects a deleted key is rejected. The same history is
serialized to the operation-history format a recognized register/set checker
consumes, so the full linearizability verdict runs in a separate privileged job
rather than pulling a JVM into the merge gate (ADR-0041, ADR-0016).

The change is a consumer of the existing surface: it drives the landed
observable S3 client over the real PUT/GET/DELETE wire and leaves the gateway,
metadata store, and wire untouched. It builds on
8af8e974e1130073a774ccdf6264a26f962bc771, which shipped the networked
observable client and history recorder this workload extends.

Fixes #406
