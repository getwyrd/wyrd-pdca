custodian: end finished multipart uploads and release their slot

An aborted multipart upload never left the store. Once its retirement
obligations drained, its upload record, its part slots, its leftover
staging entries and its admission slot all stayed. The admission count
only went up, so after enough aborted uploads every new Create would be
refused with 503. A dead upload holding a damaged record (a placement of
the wrong length, or a staging entry that will not decode) was worse: it
was held for ever, together with every fragment of its chunk.

The custodian's retirement leg now finishes these uploads:

- It walks an aborted or completed upload's leftover staging entries,
  marking every fragment each one names before deleting it. The walk is
  bounded per pass and resumes where it stopped.
- For an aborted upload only, a damaged part or staging record is
  retired by chunk id: every fragment of that chunk the fleet's listings
  find is marked first, then the record is deleted. This runs only on a
  pass that sees the whole operator-configured fleet, the same gate GC
  already uses; otherwise the record stays held. A part record that will
  not decode names no chunk, so it is held and reported as needing a
  human.
- When nothing of the upload is left, one batch deletes the upload
  record, its slots and, if no segment adopted it, its segment-group
  marker, and lowers the admission count by exactly one. The batch
  requires the exact upload record and ledger it read, so two custodians
  racing it release the slot once.

A completed upload keeps its record, so an identical Complete retry
still answers AlreadyCompleted. The new tombstone input on the drain
context decides when it may go; the deployed custodian passes "retain"
until the tombstone window is measured (#625).

One gap is stated rather than hidden: a write still landing after the
listing, at a position a damaged staging entry does not name, is kept on
disk for good. It is never lost, but nothing reclaims it automatically.

Builds on the retirement drain of #1061 and #1062.
