dst: cover the restore session fence with seeded race tests

After a metadata restore, the post-restore pass closes upload sessions
that came back from the older image and records who owes the cleanup of
their data. That step races any client still writing the same session,
and its records lead to deletes. Until now it was only tested with
scripted in-process orderings, never under the seeded simulation the
project requires for new destructive or concurrent paths.

This adds three seeded properties to the DST campaign, run against the
real restore pass over the simulated TiKV store:

- A concurrent writer (a client finishing an open upload, or an upload
  publishing its object) lands at a seed-drawn moment during the pass.
  Exactly one of the two wins. The winner's state and cleanup records
  are all present and checked by payload, and the loser leaves nothing
  behind. When the writer wins, the pass names the session as changed
  under it, never as a cleanup-key collision.
- The pass's own commit is answered "outcome unknown" and is applied
  whole or not at all. The pass reports the error after its other
  findings, and the next pass leaves the session closed with exactly one
  set of cleanup records.
- A walk over every landing point shows, for both session shapes, a run
  where the writer slipped in between the pass's read and its commit, so
  the race window is reached rather than assumed.

The properties were shown to fail when the production fence is broken
on purpose: with its check on the session bytes dropped, with its state
change and cleanup records split into two commits, and with a lost
commit diagnosed by cleanup key before the session is re-read. No
production code changes.
