custodian: fence completing upload sessions after a metadata restore

Restoring the metadata store from an older image can bring back an
upload that was mid-finish ("Completing") when the image was taken, even
though it was rolled back or torn down since. Its data may already be
reclaimed, yet the session can still publish an object over it. The
segment records its finish attempt wrote also have no deleter, so they
leak. The post-restore pass named such a session but did not fence it.

The pass now fences a Completing session the same way it fences an Open
one: one commit moves it to Aborting at the next epoch and installs two
retire obligations, one owing all of its parts and staged data, one
owing the segment records of the attempt it was in. Neither obligation
may overwrite one already in the store; a taken key leaves the session
untouched and named for a human.

The bytes obligation owes the whole session rather than a list of part
numbers, as the multipart design proposal still says. A sparse list
does not fit the store's value limit: 10,000 alternating part numbers
encode to about 129 KB against a 100 KB ceiling, and FoundationDB would
refuse the batch on every run, leaving every later session unfenced.
Owing the whole session covers the same records, because no part can
commit once a session leaves Open.

After a fence, and on every later run, the pass reads the attempt's
segment records. Any record that does not decode, is not a segment key
of that attempt, or names a chunk no part holds gets the session named
as needing a human, in the report, the CLI verdict and the audit log. A
re-run also names an already-fenced session whose records obligation is
missing, undecodable, or owes anything but that attempt's segments.
