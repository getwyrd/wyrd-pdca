core: settle a part commit whose outcome the store could not report

When the metadata store cannot say whether a part commit was applied (a
TiKV commit RPC that timed out, say), the attempt used to stop at once
with an in-doubt error. A client then saw an in-doubt answer even for a
part that had been saved, and had to upload it again.

The attempt now re-reads the part, the upload and its own slot, in at
most two laps of four reads, and answers Committed only when it finds
its own slot gone while a session read made after that slot read still
finds the open upload at the exact bytes the commit required. While the
upload is open, only this attempt's own commit removes its slot, so no
other writer can produce that evidence. A part record names no attempt,
and an empty part's bytes match another attempt's, so the part itself is
never taken as proof.

Anything else keeps the in-doubt error, which now says whether the
commit may still land and names what ended the reads. Each read is
weighed as it returns, so a read that fails later keeps what earlier
reads showed: once a read finds the upload moved to a newer epoch, or a
rival part where the commit required none, the error reports that the
commit can no longer land.

After an unknown outcome the attempt sends nothing more: no second
commit and no compensation. A late staging batch of the same attempt can
put its slot back at the very bytes the commit required, and a
same-number rival can write an empty prior's bytes again, so no read can
tell a commit that did not land from one that landed and was then
rebuilt. A resent commit could then land twice, and a compensation could
schedule a saved part's data for deletion. The cost is availability: such
an attempt answers in doubt and its slot stays held until the upload
ends.

This builds on #1028. Nothing calls this code in production until the S3
UploadPart verb is wired (#669), so clients see no change yet.
