custodian: protect staged multipart bytes from GC and restore

A multipart upload's bytes are durable long before the upload is published: a
committed part is named by its `part:` record, and a still-streaming part by
its owned `sidx:` staging entries. Neither of those is a committed chunk map,
so garbage collection's reference set never saw them, and reclaimed such a
fragment the moment it carried a stale `orphan:` mark. The post-restore pass
had the same blind spot: it marked a live upload's fragments as stranded, and
the next GC pass deleted them. A user uploading a large object in parts could
lose in-flight data to routine maintenance, with no error at upload time.

This adds a staged protection class, read by the two passes that actually
delete or mark bytes (GC's reclaim and the post-restore mark gate) and by
them alone — scrub and drain-status keep reading only committed chunk maps,
so their behaviour, faults and read cost are unchanged. The class covers
every session listed under `mpu:`, in every state, read through each
session's own bounded key ranges (never a namespace-wide scan), in the order
that keeps a chunk protected across a part commit or a publication landing
mid-read: owned entries, then committed parts, then committed inodes. A
record this reading cannot parse makes the class incomplete and blocks both
passes fleet-wide until it is repaired, matching the existing rule for an
unreadable committed chunk map; a record whose placement cannot be trusted
holds its whole chunk; a store fault fails the pass outright. The
post-restore command's summary and its NEEDS-HUMAN paragraph, and the
matching runbook entry, now name a staged multipart record distinctly from a
committed object, so an operator knows which kind of record to go repair.

Restore's protection covers the upload records already durable when it read
them; a write or upload that starts mid-pass is a known follow-up, not
covered here.
