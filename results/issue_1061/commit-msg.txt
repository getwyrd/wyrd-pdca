core,custodian: drain multipart part and segment records

Completing a multipart upload, or rolling a completion attempt back, leaves a
retirement obligation in the `retire:` ledger: a promise that the upload's
`part:`/`psum:` records, or the attempt's `seg:` records, will be removed.
Nothing drained those obligations, so the records stayed in the metadata
store for ever and grew with every upload.

The custodian now drains `retire:records:` obligations on every
reconstruction pass of `reconcile_step`. Each step deletes one bounded batch
of records and writes its progress back into the obligation itself, as an
optional cursor, in the same commit. The step that deletes the last record
deletes the obligation. Records mode only deletes; it never writes an
`orphan:` mark. `retire:bytes:` obligations are left untouched for the next
change.

A pass does a fixed amount of work whatever the ledger's size: at most 64
obligations visited, 70 store reads and 17 commits. It resumes where the
previous pass stopped. An obligation that cannot progress (it will not
decode, its cursor is out of range, its advanced value would cross
MAX_VALUE_BYTES, or the store refuses its commit) is held, unchanged, with
an audit event. It still counts against the pass's budget, so it cannot
starve the rest of the ledger, and an over-size value is never sent to the
store, which FoundationDB would refuse for good. A pass reports the ledger
as done only when it read all of it, head to end, and found nothing owed.

A `{seg}` group is finished only when a read of its whole range finds it
empty, because the obligation does not record where the range ends. A page
the store cut short is followed, never taken for the end.

To leave room for the cursor, a Complete now refuses an obligation larger
than MAX_VALUE_BYTES less the cursor's widest encoding (69 bytes). An
obligation without a cursor encodes exactly as before. A build from before
this change cannot decode an obligation that carries a cursor: its GC is
unaffected (it never decodes obligations), but its restore check reports
such a value as undecodable. The runtime and building-block docs say so.

Fixes #1061
