## Round 2 — one partial decline

The ten findings of round 2's `review-batch.md` are all **fixed** except the sub-clause below,
which is declined on its merits (the finding's main claim — that a batch could exceed the
envelope — IS fixed: the split now charges [`batch_bytes`] over the whole assembled batch and
fails closed with `ChunkMapError::BatchOverBudget`).

**WITHDRAWN in round 5 — the decline below was wrong and the finding is now FIXED.** The
operation cap is not an invented number: proposal 0016 states it normatively — "`B` is
therefore `min(B_bytes, B_ops)` … an operation-derived cap `B_ops` calibrated so that a
batch's sequential round trips fit the transaction deadline … every row of the batch
inventory whose mutation or precondition **count** can grow is bounded by both"
(`0016:640-648`). Round 5 implements it: `MAX_BATCH_OPS` (`crates/core/src/metadata.rs:326`),
charged by `batch_ops` (`:2964`), applied by the split (`batch_ranges`, `:3151`) and by the
unsplittable flip (`:2765`), with `ChunkMapError::BatchOverOps` (`:593`) and the test
`a_batch_is_bounded_by_operations_as_well_as_bytes` (`:4339`). The row is kept for the
record; it no longer declines anything.

crates/core/src/metadata.rs:2373 | BUG | or an operation-count cap | The byte half of this finding is fixed (`staged_batches` now charges the WHOLE assembled batch — every precondition, put and delete — re-splits against the caller's measured contribution, and fails closed with `BatchOverBudget` rather than emitting an over-envelope transaction; `flip_batch` does the same for the unsplittable flip). The **operation-count** half is declined: the `MetadataStore` seam states its operational envelope purely in bytes and seconds — "10 KB key, 100 KB value, 10 MB and 5 s per transaction" (`crates/traits/src/lib.rs:747-752`) — and declares no operation-count limit for any backend in play. A cap here would be a number no backend contract supplies, that no test could calibrate against a real limit, and that would silently diverge the moment a backend's real limit differed. Every operation is already charged for the key bytes it contributes, so an operation-heavy batch is charged for being operation-heavy. If a backend is later found to impose a hard op count, that belongs on the trait's envelope contract (and its conformance suite) first, not as a private constant in the segment committer.

## Round 5 — one class, declined on the flat peer (five findings)

All five findings of round 5's `review-batch.md` are the **same** finding in three
spellings: the segmented repoint's compare-and-swap adopts a destination without
`require_absent(desired:dserver:<target>)`, so a concurrent drain of that destination could
be declared `Satisfied` and wiped between the repoint's write and its CAS.

**Declined: this is pre-existing behaviour of the path the segmented arm mirrors, not
something this slice introduces, and the slice makes it no worse.** On the target base
(`origin/main` @ `9120f7a`) the **flat** evacuation CAS carries exactly
`require(inode == prior)` plus the displaced fragments' `orphan:` puts and **no destination
fence at all** (`crates/custodian/src/rebalance.rs:274-296` on the base — read it: the batch
is built at `:292-296` and nothing in it names `desired:`); reconstruction's flat repoint is
the same shape (`crates/custodian/src/reconstruction.rs:556-585` on the base). The segmented
arm routes through the *same* builder with a **stricter** precondition set — it adds
`require(seg == prior)` and `require(inode == prior_root)` where the flat arm has only the
root CAS (`crates/core/src/metadata.rs:2480-2485`) — so a drain race that is reachable on
the segmented path is reachable on the flat path today, on `main`, without this slice.

Fixing it here would mean designing the destination-drain fence for the whole maintenance
plane inside a record-shape slice: the fence is one half of X47, whose other half is the
retirement drain re-reading each segment's **current** placement at drain time
(`0016:2416-2430`) — the `retire:bytes:{generation}` obligation the multipart session slice
owns. The two only close the race together; a destination fence shipped alone changes which
interleaving loses without closing any of them, and it would change the flat path's
long-standing behaviour as a side effect of a slice whose stated scope excludes the drain
protocol. Same disposition and same reason as this file's round-1 rows. Deferred: #636/#637;
recorded here rather than fixed, and confirmed with the maintainer at Plan (2026-07-27).
