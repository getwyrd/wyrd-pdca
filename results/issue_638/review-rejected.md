crates/chunkstore-fs/src/lib.rs:412 | BUG | retaining the fragment and merely returning | Rejected: this asks the store to **remove** the bytes of a publication it could not time, and `rename(2)` admits no predicate and cannot be cancelled — a store can only judge *before* it or retract *after* it. Retraction was implemented in **iteration 3** and rejected by review on two independent grounds (`results/issue_638/iteration-v3/check-review.md`): C5 — "a crash can leave late bytes even though `WriteDeadlineExpired` promises 'not applied'"; T3 — "the pre-rename existence snapshot races same-id puts and deletes … can retract another acknowledged writer's fragment". Making it crash-safe needs a durable publication marker plus open-time recovery: ≈100 lines of new production code in the hot write path (≈35 to arm/disarm the marker, ≈40 to teach the open-time reaper to resolve markers — it unlinks `*.tmp` and collects empty chunk directories today, `crates/chunkstore-fs/src/lib.rs:134-162` — and ≈25 plus a `#[cfg(unix)]` arm for inode-precise retraction), 2–3 extra syscalls per deadline-carrying write, and a **new hazard class**: startup recovery that *deletes published `.frag` files*. It buys one turtle — the marker's own confirm step is equally unbounded. The trade is explicit: bytes that did land late are garbage their position's `orphan:` evidence already covers (`0016:1547-1550`, position coverage), while an erased live fragment is unrecoverable. **What the store does instead** is not *claim* the publication was late: it reports `WriteEffect::Unknown` — "I could not verify this landed in window" — which is exactly what a clock read taken after a syscall evidences (`crates/traits/src/lib.rs:871-892`, `:958`), classifying `Indeterminate` so no caller is told "nothing happened" over possibly-durable state, and (iteration 7) telling that caller to **re-read** rather than to re-authorize (`crates/traits/src/lib.rs:1003-1012`). `Ok(())` still means *published strictly before the deadline*, checked at both ends (`crates/chunkstore-fs/src/lib.rs:373`, `:417`); deleting the post-publication read makes `crates/chunkstore-fs/tests/conformance.rs:698` and `crates/dst/tests/network.rs:959` red (verified).
crates/chunkstore-fs/src/lib.rs:417 | BUG | cannot restore the already-reclaimed evidence | Same rejection as the `:412` row above, on the same evidence: the remedy demanded is retraction of the published bytes, which is not atomic with the rename (a crash in between leaves exactly what the retraction claims to have removed) and which deletes by *path*, so it can destroy a concurrent same-id writer's already-acknowledged fragment — trading an uncertain write for certain data loss. The ≈100-line crash-safe alternative and its new startup-deletion hazard are costed in that row.
crates/chunkstore-fs/src/lib.rs:389 | BUG | The unconditional rename can be delayed until after the deadline | The `rename` itself, recorded here so the same rejection matches when the finding lands on the publishing call rather than on the check after it. Reason as in the `:412` row: prevention is the pre-publication refusal (`:373`), which is the last instant a store *can* refuse — the D server's accept queue, the blocking pool's queue, the chunk-directory creation and the data write are all behind it. The remaining interval is one `rename(2)` on an already-written file inside one directory, which no filesystem lets a caller make conditional on time; "withholding acknowledgement" is not a weaker substitute for preventing it but the only honest report a store can make about a step it neither bounds nor cancels. The alternative — publish, then unlink — is strictly worse in both crash-safety and concurrency, and is costed in the `:412` row.
crates/chunkstore-grpc/src/client.rs:253 | CONVENTION | does not bound the external RPC await from `deadline_millis` | Deliberate, and the *opposite* of what this slice is for. (1) **Standing rejection.** A duplicate caller-side bound on the fragment-write path was reviewed and rejected in this repo already — `results/issue_508/review-rejected.md:10`, on the grounds that the `ChunkStore` implementation bounds the network via `connect_with_timeout`; the brief for this slice restates that "that rejection stands and is not reopened" and cites 0016 `:1557-1564` for why a caller-only bound is the insufficient half. The production composition does wire it: `crates/server/src/cli.rs:1441` dials every D-server client with `GrpcChunkStore::connect_with_timeout`, so every RPC on that channel — all five methods, not just `put_fragment` — is timeout-bounded at the composition, which is where the channel's lifetime is owned. Adding a per-call bound to one of the five would be an asymmetry with no safety content. (2) **A bound at the deadline would destroy the verdict this slice adds.** The value of a server-enforced deadline to a caller is that the refusal is *definite*: `WriteDeadlineExpired` classifies terminal and says the write was not applied. A client that cancels *at* `deadline_millis` instead observes tonic's `CANCELLED`, which the seam classifies `ErrorClass::Transient` (`client.rs:51-57`) — i.e. "may be worth retrying, may still land": strictly less information, and it would make leg B of `tests/write_deadline.rs`, `tests/round_trip.rs` and `crates/dst/tests/network.rs` unobservable through the trait client. (3) **It would not even prevent a late landing**, so it cannot substitute for the fix: when a tonic client resets the stream, the server drops the request future, but the store's `spawn_blocking` closure (`crates/chunkstore-fs/src/lib.rs:303`) is *not* cancelled and runs on to the rename. That is precisely why enforcement in this patch sits at the publish point inside that closure (`crates/chunkstore-fs/src/lib.rs:340-388`), where a cancelled-but-still-running write is refused. The await-discipline MUST is satisfied where it can be — the channel timeout at composition — and the new field's contribution is the acceptor-side half no caller bound can provide. Documented in the code at `crates/chunkstore-grpc/src/client.rs:243-262` so the decision travels with the seam, not only with this bundle.
