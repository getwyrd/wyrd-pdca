custodian: reap multipart uploads whose client went silent

A multipart upload that a client starts and then abandons (no Complete,
no Abort) used to stay Open for ever. Its staged parts stay protected,
its admission slot is never given back, and once MAX_SESSIONS such
uploads pile up every new CreateMultipartUpload gets 503 SlowDown with
no way out short of editing the store by hand.

The custodian now runs a reaper over Open sessions as part of every
reconcile_step, so the deployed loop reaps on each interval. A session
is fenced to Aborting, and then torn down by the existing retirement
drain, when either:

- it has made no progress for W_open (7 days) and holds no live slot or
  staging lease: progress is the newest of its creation time, its part
  commit stamps and its slot reservations, read slot-first, and the
  fence pins the slots it read free; or
- it is older than the W_session residency ceiling (30 days), however
  live it looks.

The reaper refuses to act at all, and says so, when the stored
admission profile does not match its own, or the ledger is missing.
Sessions stamped under a clock the reaper does not own are never judged
by age, but their teardown still runs. Each fence is the one shared
abort fence batch, checked against MAX_BATCH_OPS and MAX_BATCH_BYTES
before it is sent.

Operators get a one-shot, `wyrd custodian --reap-abandoned-uploads`: one
metadata-only pass with no --endpoints, exiting non-zero when a session
is held, a fence outcome is unknown, or the profile check refuses. It
opens only a store that already exists (new
RedbMetadataStore::open_existing), so a wrong --data-dir or an empty
meta.redb is refused instead of reported clean, and its last line names
the store it ran on.

Known gap, documented in the reaper module and the runtime view: a slot
reserved and released between the reaper's reads and its fence is not
seen. Closing it needs a change to proposal 0016; its DST regression is
checked in and ignored until then.

Fixes #1015
