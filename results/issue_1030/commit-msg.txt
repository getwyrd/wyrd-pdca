core: settle a part compensation whose outcome is unknown

When a part commit is refused or stopped, the attempt sends a compensation:
one batch that releases its slot and hands its chunks to a retirement
obligation. If the store answered that batch "outcome unknown", the attempt
stopped in doubt without reading anything, even when the compensation had
landed. A client got an in-doubt error for a refusal or stop that in fact
completed, and a commit RPC timeout on that path would turn every refusal
(a ceiling, a same-number rival, a rollback) into an error the client cannot
act on.

Such a compensation is now settled from reads alone, by the rule the commit
in doubt already follows, and is never sent again. It gets the route's normal
answer only on evidence no other batch can produce: for a part with chunks,
its own obligation at the exact bytes that round put, keyed under the epoch
the round required; for an empty part, its slot gone and a session read made
after that still at the bytes the round required. A slot still held, an
obligation key found empty and the clock are never evidence. Otherwise the
answer stays in doubt, and the "may still commit" flag falls only on a read
that shows the round can never land (the session moved, or it was deleted:
no record, and a slot read after that finds the slot gone).

This also closes a false success in the commit's own proof. A first upload
whose slot key was erased by damage was answered Committed with no part
record. The proof now reads the part key between the slot read and the last
session read, and answers Committed only if a decodable record is there.

Three limits of the proof stay, each reached only through damage, and are
documented in the module doc and the architecture doc. No public type
changes, no record format changes, no new writes.
