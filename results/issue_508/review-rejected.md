crates/server/src/lib.rs:183 | BUG | Multipart is exposed with the reaper disabled and only a warning | **Settled by the plan, not by this patch.** The brief's *Open questions* §3 states the posture verbatim: "The startup signal for a missing reaper is a **warn-level operator signal in this slice**. If the maintainer wants a hard startup refusal instead, say so at sign-off — it is a one-line posture change, not a redesign." The finding is therefore a decision already taken by the human who owns it, and re-taking it inside Do would overrule the plan. What the finding names as the harm — "abandoned `Open`/`Completing` sessions and completed tombstones without their required exits until admission is permanently exhausted" — is also no longer exhaustible in this iteration's shape: a `Completed` tombstone returns its **staged-admission** slot as soon as it stops owning staged records (`release_admission_batch`, `crates/core/src/multipart.rs:2069`, teardown arm at `:2840-2857`), so the ≈70-lifetime-upload ceiling the finding descends from is gone; what a missing reaper still costs is the `mpu:` tombstone population, bounded by `max_records` (≈65 K) and drained by #625's `W_tombstone`. The ordered hand-off to #625 is the brief's `Production reach` clause, and the three-bundle merge order is its `Ordering note` — both maintainer-settled. If the maintainer prefers the hard refusal, it is one line in `Gateway::warn_if_reaper_absent`'s caller.

crates/core/src/write.rs:969 | CONVENTION | The new D-server fragment fan-out has no timeout | The bound belongs to the `ChunkStore` implementation that owns the network, and it exists: the production composition dials every D server through `GrpcChunkStore::connect_with_timeout` (`crates/server/src/cli.rs:1441`, `crates/server/src/custodian.rs:146`), which applies a per-request **and** connect timeout to the channel (`crates/chunkstore-grpc/src/client.rs:169-190`). `core`'s fan-out is generic over the seam and deliberately takes time as a parameter (ADR-0009), so a second timeout here would duplicate a bound the seam already enforces and would need a clock `core` does not own. The finding is also not specific to the staged path: the identical `try_join_all` fan-out is the base's own shape at `crates/core/src/write.rs:244` and `:440`, neither of which this slice touches. **What this iteration did change:** the fan-out's *failure* path returns the partial staging (`StageFailure::committed`), so a stalled-then-failed fragment write no longer strands its `sidx:` entry, its fragments or its slot — the concrete harm the finding named is closed even though the timeout itself is declined.

crates/server/src/multipart.rs:163 | CONVENTION | The detached drain has neither a time bound nor an owned abort handle, and one "pass" walks the entire unbounded retirement namespace | **Two thirds fixed, the wall-clock third rejected.** Fixed in iteration 5: the handle is owned by the gateway and aborted on drop (`DrainTasks`, `crates/server/src/lib.rs:127-160`), and one pass is bounded on both axes (`DRAIN_PAGES_PER_STEP` pages × `DRAIN_BATCHES_PER_PASS` batches per obligation). Fixed in iteration 6: `UploadPart` no longer runs the whole-namespace drain at all — a re-upload drains its **own** obligation by key (`mp::drain_one`), so no request's latency is coupled to an unrelated backlog. Still rejected: the **wall-clock** time bound. `wyrd-core` takes time as a parameter and `clippy.toml` denies a bare `SystemTime::now()` (wyrd#619), so a deadline inside the drain would be a new clock read with no lifecycle owning it; bounding the *work* is the enforceable form of the same property, and it is what the drain does.
