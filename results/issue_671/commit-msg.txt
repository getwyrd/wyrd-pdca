s3: refuse a single PUT too large for one chunk map, from its headers

A single PUT is stored as one flat chunk map, and a flat map holds at most
158 chunk references. So the largest object one PUT can store is 158 times
the gateway's --chunk-size: 158 MiB at the default 1 MiB, about 2.47 GiB at
the 16 MiB maximum. Nothing checked this. On FoundationDB a larger PUT
uploaded every byte and then failed at commit. On redb it published a map
past the size every backend is budgeted for.

A PUT whose declared length is over that limit now gets 400 EntityTooLarge
from its headers, before any of its body is read or written. The message
names the limit in bytes. It does not point at multipart, which this
gateway still answers 501.

S3 requires a PUT to declare its length, and the AWS SDKs and CLI always
do. The gateway now requires it too: Content-Length for a plain body,
x-amz-decoded-content-length for every aws-chunked variant (before, it was
read only for the -TRAILER forms, and optional there). A PUT with neither,
or a Transfer-Encoding body with no Content-Length, gets 411
MissingContentLength. A streamed upload is held to its declared length
while it streams: it is refused as soon as a chunk would pass it, not after
the whole body, and still refused if it ends short.

The chunk size stays a deployment setting. This does not pick a chunk size
per PUT, as proposal 0016 first planned: that would turn --chunk-size into
a minimum, and 5 GiB / 158 is past the 16 MiB maximum anyway.

The declared length now crosses the ObjectGateway seam, so the server
checks the limit itself on the streamed and the buffered PUT paths, and
holds any front door's body to what it declared. The single-PUT limit is
derived in wyrd-core beside the multipart part limit, from one rule, and
compile-time checks pin it under S3's 5 GiB and keep the multipart object
limit above 10 GiB at the smallest chunk size.

The --chunk-size help, the M4 blueprint and draft proposal 0016 now state
these limits, with multipart's as future ones. Streaming test harnesses
now send the decoded length the way the SDKs do, and tests whose objects
passed their fixture's new limit were resized so each still fails or
passes for its own reason. A new test,
crates/server/tests/s3_put_object_ceiling.rs, covers the boundary over the
wire and the buffered path.

Fixes #671
